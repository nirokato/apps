In loving memory of

Google Reader

October 7, 2005July 1, 2013

“All items read.”

Flowers, candles, stones, a cup of coffee. Bring what you would bring a friend.

Tap the grass where you want to leave it.

For nearly seven years and nine months it fetched the whole web every morning and handed it over in one quiet list. Then it was retired, and a lot of us never quite found the way back. This is a place to leave something for it.

    What it left behind

    Addresses that used to mean something. The struck-through ones no longer answer. You can still copy them.

    Where it lived

    • http://www.google.com/reader/view/

      Home. Most people typed reader.google.com and let it redirect.

    • http://www.google.com/reader/view/#stream/user%2F-%2Fstate%2Fcom.google%2Freading-list

      All items. The user/-/ part meant "whoever is signed in", which felt like being recognized.

    • http://www.google.com/reader/shared/<your-id>

      Your public Shared items page, until October 2011. Small communities formed around these.

    The API nobody was promised

    • http://www.google.com/reader/atom/feed/http://xkcd.com/atom.xml?n=20

      Any feed on the web, served back as Atom from Reader's own archive. It often remembered posts the original feed had long dropped.

    • http://www.google.com/reader/api/0/unread-count?output=json

      Where the number came from. It stopped counting at 1,000 and just said 1000+.

    • http://www.google.com/reader/api/0/stream/contents/user/-/state/com.google/starred?n=1000

      Everything you ever starred, as JSON. Swap starred for reading-list, broadcast (shared) or read.

    • http://www.google.com/reader/public/atom/user/<your-id>/state/com.google/broadcast

      Your shared items as an Atom feed, so that friends could subscribe to your taste.

    • http://www.google.com/reader/subscriptions/export

      One click, one google-reader-subscriptions.xml. The door was always unlocked.

    Still answering

    • http://googlereader.blogspot.com/feeds/posts/default

      The Official Google Reader Blog, as Atom. Its last entry is titled "A final farewell". Add ?alt=rss for RSS.

    • https://reader.apps.andymolenda.com/feed.atom

      This altar, as Atom (RFC 4287, published two months after Reader launched).

    • https://reader.apps.andymolenda.com/feed.rss

      This altar, as RSS 2.0.

    A short life

    • Oct 7, 2005Launches in Google Labs, built by a small team around Chris Wetherell.
    • Sep 28, 2006Rewritten from the ground up: the river of news, unread counts, sharing.
    • Sep 2007Graduates from Labs. Search finally arrives.
    • Jun 2008Someone hides a ninja behind the Konami code.
    • Oct 31, 2011Sharing, friends and notes are removed in favor of Google+.
    • Mar 13, 2013"Powering down Google Reader." The web objects, loudly.
    • Jul 1, 2013Retired. The servers go quiet a little after midnight that night, Pacific time.
    • Jul 15, 2013Takeout closes. Whatever wasn't exported is gone.

    Survived by

    Feedly, Inoreader, NewsBlur and The Old Reader, who took us in; and by the ones you can run yourself: FreshRSS, Miniflux and Tiny Tiny RSS (now a community fork). FreshRSS, Miniflux, Inoreader and The Old Reader still speak the "Google Reader API", so that old apps can keep syncing with a service that is no longer there. Also survived by RSS and Atom themselves, which were never the ones in trouble.

    A fan memorial. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Google, which made Google Reader. Offerings are chosen from fixed lists, so nothing typed by visitors is stored, and nothing about you is kept beyond the offering itself.
